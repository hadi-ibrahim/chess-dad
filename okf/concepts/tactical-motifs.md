# Overview

A **tactical motif** is a reusable attacking or winning pattern. When a move
loses material or a win, ChessMentor tags it with the motif that best explains
what was missed, so the mistake becomes a trainable drill.[^lichess-practice]

# Motif catalogue

* **Fork** — one piece attacks two or more targets at once (the knight fork is
  the canonical example).
* **Pin** — a piece is attacked through a more valuable piece behind it.
* **Skewer** — a valuable piece is attacked and forced to move, exposing the
  piece behind it.
* **Discovered attack** — moving one piece uncovers an attack by another.
* **Back-rank mate** — a king is mated on its home rank by a rook or queen.
* **Hanging piece** — a piece is left undefended and can be captured for free.
* **Missed mate** — a forced checkmate was available and not played.
* **Missed capture** — a free or favorable capture was available and not played.

# How to train

Solve themed puzzle sets for the motif you miss most often, then replay the
exact game position and find the best move without the engine. Spaced repetition
re-serves your own missed positions until the pattern is automatic.

[^lichess-practice]: Lichess's practice module groups tactics by theme;
  ChessMentor uses an equivalent motif taxonomy to tag its own puzzles.
